### From the primary corpus: the continuation and reversion phases

**What a continuation is.** The first trade of every session is a trade in the direction of the opening candle, read as the net of the overnight orders that execute at the open: ("the first trade I take is a continuation of the influx of volume, usually because it's just a sum of the overnight orders that are executed on the opening candle, and it creates a very good bias." — JJ, UVKVSWKFlvo ~0:56, L60). His own definitions of both words: ("by reversion, I just mean coming back to the opening price. And by continuation, I just mean like this this series of green candles or the series of gray candles for this continuation." — JJ, 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~18:10, L632). The direction rule is the colour of the opening 1-minute candle and nothing else: ("The only way you want to look for a continuation is short because the first candle was red, which means the overnight orders were mostly short. So, you're going to look for shorts." — JJ, 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~11:30, L412); ("the entry criterion for continuation is practically just the color of the opening candle. Because I'm taking an unfair trade, i.e., a move away from the fair price, I want to get in and out literally as quickly as possible." — JJ, KN7j6NXXAio ~5:28, L235); ("even though high time frame bias is short, opening candle is green, so I only like to take continuations in the opening candle direction." — JJ, gjWiGBiVis0 ~4:49, L186). From August 2026 he also reads the opening 1-second candle: ("Um, 38 points as usual, green opening candle, uh, green 1 second candle, structure breakout, shift, everything you need for a long continuation seems to be in place." — JJ, g68HHbrIBBg ~4:22, L187); ("1 second green, 1 minute green, clear break of structure, clear displacement. High time frame is short, but obviously you can go against that if the opening candle is so strong." — JJ, PN1UKQMPb5M ~0:42, L51); ("The opening candle wasn't too biased. There were pretty large wicks on both sides, but uh opening 1 second was red." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:27:29, L2747).

**The trigger inside the continuation.** The colour alone is not an entry; the candle (or the next one) must close through prior structure: ("So the first trade literally the color of the first candle as long as it breaks structure of the previous candles then I'm good then I'm good to enter and I will do a 38 point take profit and a 25point stop-loss for this trade." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~4:31, L210); ("There is no structural break, so there are no continuations until the first one we get here." — JJ, 0uAQUHEB_L8 ~16:57, L636); ("opening candle is green, but it's not closing above any structure. So, I am long biased, marking out the pre-open price as the fair price, but I'm not going to take continuations because it didn't close above any structure. Not going to short because the opening was green." — JJ, gjWiGBiVis0 ~8:34, L308). A wick that rejects the opening direction, or two large wicks, cancels the trade or defers it to the second candle: ("there's a a huge wick rejecting shorts and it means the open's fairly priced so I'm just not going to take anything for a continuation on this day." — JJ, EyR3i2GxwyA ~2:38, L117); ("I didn't go into the opening candle because the wicks were quite large on both sides. So I waited for the second candle to close. It closed below the first candle, which was great." — JJ, pwO9IAu7FUQ ~10:31, L374). A tiny opening body means no bias and no continuation: ("the first candle was extremely unbiased because the body was literally four points. So, that alone just tells me that the market doesn't really have a strong bias going into the session" — JJ, xTxrtFKlQfc ~2:58, L128). Follow-up continuation entries are allowed on each further break inside the window: ("And remember to follow up within the first 5 minutes whenever it breaks the structure in your favor." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~36:24, L1234).

**How long it lasts and what ends it.** Two conditions coexist. The time condition is five minutes: ("So, I'll take a continuation away from the fair price. After that, which is just the first 5 minutes. After that, I'm only looking for a reversal. And the reversal takes the next 85 minutes to complete a 90-minute session in total." — JJ, KN7j6NXXAio ~2:28, L127); ("I usually don't take them after the first 5 minutes. This is in the sixth minute." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~30:43, L1046); ("it's obviously too late to take continuations now because the original unfair move never happened. And if it doesn't happen within the first 5 minutes, it's not happening." — JJ, BLvsYJ4sqn8 ~1:47, L87). The volume condition is the end of the "unfair move": ("when the market's going to open, there's going to be an initial unfair move which creates a continuation. In June 2026 the time limit was fifteen minutes: ("technically a continuation trade as well just because the opening candle was red and we're still within 15 minutes." — JJ, gjWiGBiVis0 ~6:22, L236); ("it is 16 minutes after the open I usually stop continuations after 15 minutes" — JJ, xTxrtFKlQfc ~10:50, L390; `stops_psychology.json`). ### From the primary corpus: structure breaks, displacement candles, grading and entry timing

**Structure and its break, in his words.** Structure is a single-candle wick extreme: a wick lower (or higher) than the candle before and the candle after. ("it's basically whenever there's a wick just like that, and it's lower than these two. So, it's structure. Just wait for a close below it." — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~10:18, L299); ("And structure is when a wick is taller than the two next to it, which we just get right here." — JJ, gjWiGBiVis0 ~3:21, L138); ("When there's a wick down here that is lower than the two candles previous, the candle before and the candle after. If the wick is lower and then a candle comes in here and it breaks the structure, then I will take a short position." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~6:54, L283); ("if you have a wick here and this candle breaks above the wick because this one is taller than the two next to it, it is structure. When this shoots up beyond it and closes, then the structure is being broken." — JJ, DEkzU8gqX-o ~6:13, L241). A monotonic run has no structure: ("There is no structure here, believe it or not, because the left one is always lower than the right one. So, no structure." — JJ, gjWiGBiVis0 ~13:29, L468). The break is a close, never a wick: ("Just wait for the structure to break. Wick, but no break. I want to close below." — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~4:36, L148); ("As long as it closes below the structure that it broke, I'm good with entering and then the stop loss take profit are static." — JJ, aCOgfvL6lK8 ~22:40, L836). A wick through a level weakens it: ("the structure is invalidated when it leaves a wick but doesn't close" — JJ, g68HHbrIBBg ~13:02, L498). Several untouched wicks at one level make the strongest break: ("There's a bunch of structure here. 1 2 3 four wicks that have not broken this level. So, it's outstanding structure. And this candle, very strong breakup structure, obviously." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:00:24, L1919). The structure must have formed before it can be broken: ("There's no structure being formed here that is broken. Structure is forming here and it's broken here." — JJ, GMDUiamqgig ~4:39, L166). A break candle with a large wick is a judgment call: ("There's a close below, but there's a huge wick. Um, I would not short that one probably." — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~10:27, L303). No fact in this group has him using the term MSB or distinguishing it from BOS; the hosts do: ("market structure break is really if price is in a downtrend and you see a close above a previous, uh, kind of wick high." — host, SNO1wqJTq5A ~2:29, L148); ("breaks in structures and market structure breaks we use pretty interchangeably" — host, SNO1wqJTq5A ~2:57, L163); ("It can It can be a BOS, chalk, or MSB." — host, JcW8Wjnw8ck ~1:52, L73; "chalk" is a mis-hearing of CHoCH).

**Displacement candle.** The definition hardened over the year. March 2026: ("Displacement candle usually, at least in my opinion the way I trade it, is a candle that's larger than the previous one and it doesn't really have any wicks." — JJ, 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~11:45, L420). July: ("Displacement candle is classified as when it's bigger than the previous one pretty much. Just very simple definition." — JJ, EyR3i2GxwyA ~5:21, L206); ("the condition for it to be a displacement candle without a doubt is that the body of the candle is larger than that of the previous one. This one is 10.0 larger, so it's an offset." — JJ, KN7j6NXXAio ~6:35, L275), with the wick close as a preference ("This one, the body is bigger, but in my opinion, it didn't close below this wick here, and that's something I would prefer it to have" — JJ, KN7j6NXXAio ~7:14, L298). August: ("There's a slightly large wick at the bottom, but the body is larger than the previous one and it closed below the wick of the previous one.

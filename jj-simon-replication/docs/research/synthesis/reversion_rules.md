# How JJ Simon places reversion stops and targets, in his own words

Source: the verified quote set for the angles `rev_stop`, `rev_target`, `bracket_scope`, `rev_selectivity`, `cont_rules` and `management`. Every quote supplied is `verbatim=true` and `speaker_is_jj=true`; host interjections are shown in square brackets and nothing below is "not JJ". Quotes are copied as captioned (auto-translated wording such as "valuation operations", "a count", "takerit" is left in and glossed in prose) and cited as video id, approximate position, transcript line and upload date from `docs/research/transcript-index.md`. Lines cited from an extraction note rather than from a verified quote are marked "reported". Read against `docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md`, `docs/MECHANISM.md` and the frozen `StrategyConfig` in `fpt/strategy.py` (Baseline 0). Where his statements conflict across videos both are shown with dates and the later one is preferred, and the text says so.

**The short answer.** The hypothesis that his real reversion uses a stop beyond structure and a target at fair value is, in his own words, the *live-account* version of the trade, and he says that version does not work on a prop account. On every prop account, evaluation or funded, the reversion stop is a fixed point count that is never moved to a chart level; 25 points is both his default and his stated floor for the New York session, and he widens it only by halving contracts. What does change between the evaluation bracket and the funded trade is not the stop but (1) the target, which on funded accounts is matched to the measured room to fair value and to what the account needs, (2) the entry trigger, which on funded accounts is a break of structure only, (3) a wait-for-a-move-away precondition, (4) a volatility switch to 50/76 that applies to reversions too, and (5) a cadence of three or four attempts a session. Baseline 0 reproduces his evaluation bracket (25/38, every displacement, 80% room rule, three-loss stop) faithfully and misses items 1 to 5. Those are the B2 candidates in Section 7.

## 1. Reversion stop placement as he states it

*1.1 The stop is a fixed point count, not a chart level, on every prop account.* Asked for his execution criteria: ("as long as it closes below the structure that it broke, I'm good with entering and then the stop loss take profit are static. So, I don't I don't really believe too much in putting stop loss above previous high, take profit at a specific previous lower whatever. Um if you want a live account, that's definitely something you should explore more, but on prop firms, it is infinitely better to use static risk in terms of like exactly 1,000, exactly 500." — JJ, -lxNWJGWtbg ~7:06, L299-311, 2026-09-18; the same answer three months earlier at aCOgfvL6lK8 ~22:40, L836-847, 2026-06-22). The reason is the drawdown rule: ("Each stop loss I use is static. I'm not going to put my stop loss at the previous low or above the previous high because that's what many strategies do. But that's not what the drawdown of a funding account does." — JJ, PMGhHCjYG5c ~17:08, L724-734, 2026-08-20). On a reversion specifically the stop is derived from the account after the target is set: ("Now, the stop loss on the other hand is going to be essentially random. It's going to be static, but again, optimized based on a prof. ... Then the stop loss, if my account is telling me to risk 500, then it's going to be at that amount of points be at 25 points." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~58:40, L1865-1875, 2026-10-04). On a reversion to the pre-news price: ("I'm not going to put it above the previous high or put it above the candle entry or put it anywhere like that. I'm just doing exactly 12.5 points or whatever whatever my account needs based on the takerit." — JJ, l6iq0ljhxIo ~13:35, L461-468, 2026-09-20; "takerit" = take profit). Reported, not among the verified quotes: asked whether he had modelled technical stop placement, "No. No. I found infinitely better to have a static stop loss" (-lxNWJGWtbg L343-344).

*1.2 The default is 25 points and 25 is the New York floor.* ("And then 25 point stop is just something I always do. So, the stop is kind of static." — JJ, gjWiGBiVis0 ~9:52, L350-355, 2026-06-15; said of that day's reversions). ("I don't really do less than 25 points. I don't think ever in the New York session. Actually, I guess I could say never. Never. Never less than 25 points in the New York session." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:11:55, L2270-2278, 2026-10-04). On funded accounts the stop comes from a short menu chosen per account: ("the most common stop losses that I end up seeing from my optimization is 25 points. [host: Yeah.] 37.5, 50, and 75. So, a few different options, but again, you have to optimize it for your specific account. So, stop loss is just going to be what it's going to be." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:00:52, L1933-1943, 2026-10-04).

Conflict, shown both ways. Stops under 25 appear twice: the 12.5-point example above (l6iq0ljhxIo, 2026-09-20, an 08:30 FOMC-day session) and the halved evaluation bracket ("If there are 15 to 30 points in my favor for the evaluation, then I will do this one for 19 points." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~9:59, L360-366, 2026-08-23; 19 target with a 12.5 stop at 1:1.5). The later statement (2026-10-04) is "Never less than 25 points in the New York session". The later one is preferred for the 09:30 session; the 12.5 cases are an 08:30 session and an August evaluation adjustment, and Section 7 carries the August rule only as a falsification arm.

*1.3 The stop is widened, never moved, and only by cutting size.* Wide opening candle, stated on a New York morning reversion: ("if the opening candle is over 25 points, I'll use a 5076 instead of a 2538. So that's a small ATR adjustment you can change. ... And with the stop-loss, I only change the contract size. My take profit and stop-loss are static." — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~13:18, L468-481, 2026-07-20). ("My usual rule for that is if the candle's over 25 points, then I don't do my 25 stop 38 profit. I would divide size by two and I'll do 50 and 76." — JJ, UVKVSWKFlvo ~1:56, L93-98, 2026-06-01). Heavy volume with a lot of room: ("Now I know I usually say 2538. There's a little discretion you can add. In this case you have 243 points in your favor. If it's over 100, then I would probably give in and place the stop at 50 points just because the volume is extremely high that day." — JJ, CzxSgYujDxs ~9:05, L319-326, 2026-06-28). ("This one was actually 50 point stop on both of these trades just because of how much volume there was. Again, 157 point candle. ... Contract size cut in half. 50 point stop, 140 take profit just off a break of this structure here." — JJ, xTxrtFKlQfc ~6:11, L232-241, 2026-06-21). Where the wider static stop lands relative to a swing is a by-product he notices, not the placement rule: ("about 76 points. So, you can do those five micros and do a 50 point stop, which gets it below that low. Probably more likely to win just because uh the stop loss is much larger." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:14:00, L2335-2341, 2026-10-04). Account size: ("I doubled the stop loss and take profit because I was on a 150k account. What I talk about usually is 25 point stop 38 point take profit. That's good for 50k accounts." — JJ, Lep139B5EbU ~2:47, L124-135, 2026-04-03).

*1.4 Two things that are not reversion stops.* The wide funded stop for a defined trend: ("Now, for entries into a funded account, I want to have a really big stop loss. ... I had a daily loss limit of $3,000. The reason I want to have a wide stop loss is because I have a defined trend." — JJ, MIWsdldzJgQ ~6:03, L230-239, 2026-07-27) and the largest-possible stop with no target ("What I will do for continuations is give myself the largest possible stop-loss." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~16:27, L575-596, 2026-10-04) belong to trend and continuation trades, and are still dollar-sized. The structure-stop, fair-value-target version is the live-account trade: ("If you're on a real account, all you have to do is buy at some point below this line; your take profit would be there and your stop loss below it. That's all you need to do on a real account, because you just have to recognize your bias to make money. In a corporate funding account, that's not going to work. Funding companies are designed to make you lose money with strategies like this" — JJ, KN7j6NXXAio ~20:24, L772-784, 2026-07-25). That is the hypothesis in the brief, and it is the thing he says he does not do on prop accounts.

## 2. Reversion target as he states it

*2.1 Evaluation: 38 points, even with more room.* ("my evaluations are mostly static. Even if I see 50 points to a fair price, I'm not going to go for 50 points in the eval. I'm only going for 38 because 38 is optimal according to my static risk-to-reward." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~39:46, L1287-1292, 2026-10-04). ("I usually, or actually always, use 3825 for my valuation operations. It's a 1:1.5, and it's the best way to pass the evaluations of funding companies." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~1:13, L69-74, 2026-08-23). The evaluation set: ("each evaluation I take will be 2538, 5076, or 12519." — JJ, KhooqEQK9bA ~9:05, L393-417, 2026-09-06; "12519" is read as 125/190 in the extraction note). Room step function, August: ("if there are 30 to 76 points in my favor for the evaluations, then I will normally only have a profit take of 38 points. If there are 15 to 30 points in my favor for the evaluation, then I will do this one for 19 points." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~9:59, L360-366, 2026-08-23). The consistency rule caps the day, not the trade: ("on Eval, there's the consistency rule. So, uh, I'm just going to go for 1500 because you don't want to win more than half of your thing in one day" — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~38:36, L1048-1057, 2026-06-10).

*2.2 Funded: the target is matched to the room and to what the account needs; the stop stays 25.* ("in continuations I'll always do 38 to 25. That should be 25. Because you don't know how many points you have in your favor. Obviously, for the reversal, you can see, okay, there's exactly like this. There are exactly 47 points in favor of my reversal. So I'll take a count that I need as close to 47 points as possible." — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~19:33, L674-683, 2026-07-20; "a count" = an account). ("Take profit. I'll do 38. I'll do 50. I'll do 100. I'll do 75. Or I'll do exactly the amount of points in my favor. So, 62 in this case." — JJ, gjWiGBiVis0 ~9:52, L350-355, 2026-06-15). ("I have some that need 75, some that need 100. Okay, usually it's like split up by 25 points." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:00:46, L1926-1937, 2026-10-04). Target above the room, stop unchanged: ("Apparently, I looked for 100. It was giving me 85 in my favor. ... I'm just going to let it run for 100. Uh, so I let that run with a 25-point stop." — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~22:54, L784-791, 2026-07-20). The distance is known before entry, so the trade is routed, not bracketed: ("if you know you're going to get 50 points, one of your prop firm accounts must exist and be the ideal account to do it" — JJ, q8PXXvY9lS0 ~5:45, L221-231, 2026-07-13). When fair cannot be located the target is a distance, not a price: ("the target isn't a specific price ... All I know is that the fair price is up, and the risk management on this account indicates that I need 150 points with one contract." — JJ, pwO9IAu7FUQ ~5:03, L195-205, 2026-08-02). The ratio: ("the risk reward on eval I do 1 to 1.5. Funded sometimes 1 to 2, 1 to 3, 1 to 4, 1 to 5, whatever." — JJ, C-_0QhKZmA4 ~16:52, L586-595, 2026-06-06). Routing by room: ("38 or less is usually my evaluation calculation. And then 38 or more, usually 50 or more, to be honest, are funded account trades, where you're looking for many more points." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~2:03, L95-106, 2026-08-23).

*2.3 The room rule: 75 to 80% of the target must lie before fair value.* ("I need 80% of my trade to be below the red line. I do not want to trade more than 20% of my trade's profit target away from fair value." — JJ, DEkzU8gqX-o ~7:16, L276-282, 2026-09-08). ("if I want to target 100 point win, then I need at least 80 points in my favor to take that trade. ... 80% 75 if I'm getting a little bit greedy is sort of the mark that I continuously go off of." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:20:59, L2548-2555, 2026-10-04). ("I'm doing the 38 point wins, which means I need 30 points." — JJ, BLvsYJ4sqn8 ~11:19, L382-385, 2026-09-17). ("she was also a little too close to go for 38. I usually want 30 points." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~19:19, L662-671, 2026-08-23). Conflict: in April the room had to exceed the target: ("my target was 75 points, and I was more than 75 points above what I believe to be the fair price, which is the pre-open candle." — JJ, Lep139B5EbU ~3:59, L159-169, 2026-04-03). The later 80% rule (September and October) is preferred.

*2.4 Layering instead of partials; no runners.* ("I send one account in all the way down to what I think fair price is, and then another account basically like halfway. Just depending on what's optimal for my specific account." — JJ, EyR3i2GxwyA ~7:01, L261-271, 2026-07-05). ("Leaving runners and partials is probably one of the worst things you can do on prop firms." — JJ, aCOgfvL6lK8 ~6:46, L300-312, 2026-06-22). Conflict with the earliest video: ("I was pretty confident at a 1 to 1 when it got back to here and then just letting it run for the rest." — JJ, 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~17:37, L614-620, 2026-03-28). The June statement is later and is preferred; the March "let it run" is not repeated anywhere in the corpus.

## 4. Reversion selectivity and cadence

*4.1 Precondition: price must first have moved away by more than the target.* ("I waited until we had a move of more than 38 points upwards so I could look for 38-point reversals. The first entry was right after the first displacement candle we got. Second entry, the next displacement candle. That one took a while to win. After that, it's too close to take reversals at 6:00 p.m. Because remember, it's 6:00 p.m. It's a fair price." — JJ, g68HHbrIBBg ~0:53, L62-73, 2026-08-09). The same shape with the halved bracket: ("basically every time it moves more than 15 points from the right price in either direction, I'm going to send a reversal." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~11:20, L405-412, 2026-08-23; reported at L932-935 he refuses 14.75 points). Note for `docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md` row 5: the 38-point band is recorded there as joetroyer's number, not his; g68HHbrIBBg gives it first-hand as "a move of more than 38 points" for "38-point reversals", that is, the band equals the target.

*4.2 Window.* ("I'll do a continuation in the first 5 minutes. After that, I will look for a mean reversion for 5 to 90 minutes. So, 5 minutes and after that, I look for the reversal. There is no maximum number of transactions." — JJ, KN7j6NXXAio ~1:45, L99-105, 2026-07-25; March had "from 10 minutes to 90 minutes", 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~2:47 L127-139; the later 5 is preferred). Hard stop: ("I'm usually done trading at 11:00 because the farther away you are from the open, the less fair that this price becomes. The first 90 minutes is always great" — JJ, 7UXiI2arAlQ ~10:41, L343-353, 2026-05-06). He states no earlier reversion cutoff anywhere in the supplied quotes.

*4.3 Cadence.* ("New York a.m. one continuation, three or four reversions. And then from there, I'll take a longer trade." — JJ, -lxNWJGWtbg ~2:46, L150-154, 2026-09-18). ("Three to four trades max. You don't want to over risk into a situation that's not playing out in your favor. If you're winning, then you can keep trading obviously. If you're losing, it's not the best to continue" — JJ, 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~2:47, L127-139, 2026-03-28). Against: "There is no maximum number of transactions." (KN7j6NXXAio, 2026-07-25). Shown both ways: the March "max" is qualified by "If you're winning, then you can keep trading"; the July statement removes the cap; the September description is three or four as the usual count. The preferred reading, by date, is no hard cap on attempts, a usual count of three or four, and the loss cap below. Loss cap: ("Personally, if I'm trying to trade reversion and I lose three in a row, then I'm done. It's just a market that's most likely not going to revert according to my statistics." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~34:24, L1124-1129, 2026-10-04).

*4.4 Entry trigger by account.* ("Evaluations as well will be all of my displacement candle entries. Funded accounts I like to save for the breakup structure just because it's a much stronger entry. But evaluations, I'll send as many of them as I possibly can off displacement candles." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~9:43, L369-378, 2026-10-04; "breakup structure" = break of structure). Grades: ("Break of structure and a close is an A+ setup. It's a lot more likely to win when that happens. Um I am trading pretty much every displacement I see just because there's a slight bit of edge that I want to capture every single day" — JJ, 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~5:27, L212-223, 2026-03-28). On a hypothetical live account: ("I would only trade breakup structure. Second of all, okay, and third, most likely only trade expected news reversions. Those are the strongest of the trades that I take. That's why I risk the most on them in my funded accounts." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~28:23, L940-951, 2026-10-04). On choppy, large-wick days he waits for a close below several stacked wicks and still requires the room: Lep139B5EbU ~3:59, L159-169 (quoted in 2.3).

*4.5 What disqualifies or re-anchors.* Too little room (2.3). Too close after a return to fair until a fresh move away (g68HHbrIBBg, 4.1). Three losses in a row (4.3). After 11:00 (4.2). A break of structure away from the open that keeps going re-anchors fair value: ("I would now be adjusting the price to reflect this consolidation here because it broke the structure and then continued to move away from the open." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~13:42, L483-487, 2026-08-23).

## 5. Management

Default is none. ("I never go break even, which might be interesting to hear, unless there is a new session opening. ... Or, if we have news coming out, then I will go break even. But, like 95% of my trades I'm not going to go break even." — JJ, -lxNWJGWtbg ~8:50, L358-365, 2026-09-18). No partials, no runners, no early close (aCOgfvL6lK8 ~6:46 L300-312, quoted in 2.4); the two-account layer replaces partials (EyR3i2GxwyA ~7:01 L261-271). Adding is a new position on another account, only when the first is well on its way: ("the only time I add trades, that is, if one is halfway to the take-profit stage, I send another, is usually if it breaks the structure in my favor while I'm still in that trade." — JJ, KN7j6NXXAio ~14:24, L556-567, 2026-07-25). The 11:00 clock stops entries, not positions: ("After 11:00 a.m., I am done trading as well, but I'll let a position play out if I'm still in one." — JJ, UVKVSWKFlvo ~7:49, L288-293, 2026-06-01). A funded reversion with a 25 stop and a 100 target is "let that run" (7KJOh2NF-lQ ~22:54 L784-791).

Continuation-only management, listed so it stays out of the reversion rules: break-even when the opening move stalls and structure breaks against the position ("I'd rather you break even than go up 300 because that will ruin your risk." — JJ, KN7j6NXXAio ~16:39, L637-646, 2026-07-25; BLvsYJ4sqn8 ~16:29 L540-547), and on the wide-stop funded drift trade a manual exit on a candle-close break of structure with no target (KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~17:57 L618-633).

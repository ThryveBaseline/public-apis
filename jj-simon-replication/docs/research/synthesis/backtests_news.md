### From the primary corpus: his backtests, sample sizes and statistics

**What a backtest is for, in his words.** The most repeated instruction in the corpus is that the statistic to backtest is the evaluation pass rate, not an equity curve: ("You need to find your pass rate. You do not need your average profit, your sharp ratio, your expected profit per trade, your win rate. That does not matter. The only thing that matters is this." — JJ, 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~35:12, L1195); ("Do not bother backtesting your live equity curve. That's not going to matter because we're trading on prop firms. All we need to know is the hit rate of the profit target before the max drawdown. So, how many times you can win before lose on the eval. That's all you need to backtest for." — JJ, wsKx7L9EJys ~2:00, L85); repeated in June (XhsbfEdJBAc ~14:06, L517), in the July recording (KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~23:31, L791) and in September (sWJa8vRfPb8 ~1:33, L80). The test must run under the firm's rules, per firm: ("Run retrospective tests, but pretend you're in a funded account environment. Make your trades, follow the drawdown limit, have a profit target and consistency rules. Just treat it that way. You will automatically discover if you will be successful in funding ventures." — JJ, AxP-cg50TdM ~16:00, L558); ("it is best to simulate it specifically for each different firm before you even try because remember, you can determine if you're going to make money on a prop firm before you buy a single eval." — JJ, 0Ua-ffltO0I ~8:09, L376). He has never tested the method on a live curve and expects it to break even there: ("If you back test it on live, I assume it's going to break even. I haven't even done it. Like there's literally no point for me to back test this thing on live because I only trade prop firms. I only need to optimize it for the pass rate." — JJ, 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~34:43, L1179); ("Don't be surprised when you use a static minus 500 plus 750 when it doesn't work on a live account because it's optimized for a prop firm." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~32:07, L1054). The pass rate is then used to infer the payout rate (XhsbfEdJBAc ~13:37, L500); the funded statistics he tracks are pass rate, average payout and average payout chance (aBVn6IUAjnM ~13:58, L469).

**The simulation recipe.** The account he simulates is a 50K evaluation with a $2,000 loss limit and a $3,000 target: ("it simulates that you have an account with a funding firm. It starts with a balance of 50,000 dollars, and if you ever lose 2,000 dollars, it means you've reached the maximum loss and your account is lost. See how many times you can reach 53,000 before dropping 2,000." — JJ, 0uAQUHEB_L8 ~2:30, L121); the same 3k/2k account in nvOaN6mRu-o (~1:58, L83). If you only try three and you lose all of them, it's not because the strategy doesn't work." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~26:14, L874); 50 again in September (0Ua-ffltO0I ~3:34, L189). ### From the primary corpus: the news trades (08:30, 10:00, 14:00 FOMC, overnight)

**The premise.** News is the one thing that moves fair price: ("News changes fair price, but opening session does not. So, you do have to be careful if some sort of news comes out and changes fair price." — JJ, 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~21:26, L740); he keeps Forex Factory on one monitor for it (GMDUiamqgig ~5:28, L191). Two kinds: ("There's two types of news. There is expected and then there is unexpected. So the expected news, this is 8:30 a.m. Red folder news EST. Unexpected is stuff like tweets, speeches, things like that." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~12:41, L460). Expected news is priced in because the print is close to the forecast: ("If the news is expected, meaning it's the 8:30 scheduled red folder news, usually happens twice a week, CPI, PPI, whatever. This is already priced in." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~13:11, L475), 90% of the time (~13:28, L484) and later 99% (~28:55, L956); ("They already have a forecast for the news. And if it doesn't deviate too much from the forecast, it will simply reverse. Even if it deviates, in the long run it still reverses.

### From the primary corpus: what fair price is

**The claim.** The method prices NQ as a basket of companies and sells or buys any move away from that price: ("it's saying that we are trying to price the futures of Nasdaq. So, we're pricing actual companies, and then any moves away from a fair price, we want to trade back to fair price with a reversion." — JJ, 8lbkj0PF1uM ~0:59, L61). Only two external events matter: ("I'm only looking at external factors that impact the price. So pretty much just news and session opens." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~3:24, L176). Of those, only news moves the fair price; a session open moves the traded price and is therefore the thing to fade: ("Fair prices only ever changed by news and by session opens. Now, session open's not going to actually change the fair price, but it'll change the real price" — JJ, UVKVSWKFlvo ~1:16, L71); ("the only thing that, in my opinion, changes the fair price is the news." — JJ, KN7j6NXXAio ~18:29, L703). Before every trade the cause of the move is classified: ("did news cause this move or was it like a session open? What caused it? Unfair, fair, news." — JJ, UVKVSWKFlvo ~1:29, L78).

**Why the open is exploitable, in his words.** ("the New York session open acts as the first major intraday reference point for fair auction. Early moves away from that price are often driven by opening flow, liquidity imbalances, stops, hedging, momentum, participation, not necessarily by a true, long-lasting repricing of the index." — JJ, 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~8:39, L318); ("when the market opens at 9:30, there is an increase in volume, all of the overnight orders are executed, institutions are acting unfairly, and price is manipulated." — JJ, 7UXiI2arAlQ ~0:44, L45); ("are we really changing the fair price of these companies just because the session opened? Well, not really," — JJ, UVKVSWKFlvo ~0:44, L53). The first seconds of the open follow the overnight order flow, which is why the continuation is taken first: ("it is pretty likely that the opening trend for at least the first 10 to 15 seconds is going to have a lot of selling orders because obviously the overnight trend was mostly bearish here." — JJ, 8lbkj0PF1uM ~5:00, L183). He sizes the edge small: ("the opening price is usually a pretty good estimate of the fair price because it 's a bias. And remember, if you have a bias in a funding firm, that's essentially a 1% advantage on average, something like that." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~8:42, L318); ("Fair value is a very strong bias that wins above break-even rates, which is why it works so well on prop firm environments." — JJ, DEkzU8gqX-o ~7:26, L282); even at fair price, ("it's a bit more likely than chance that it will go in the direction of a higher timeframe reversal, right?" — JJ, g68HHbrIBBg ~13:53, L529). Weekend gaps are not a separate rule: ("we are valuing companies and the fair value of those companies on the Nasdaq doesn't care if there was a void, because the fair price of them changed over the weekend." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~20:23, L704).

**Reference 1: the 9:29 candle body, which he equates with the 9:30 open.** The strongest single statement is the May 2026 video built around it: ("I can tell you for certain the candle is the 9:29 a.m. candle every single day. That is in EST, and it is a very, very good representation of fair price of the market." — JJ, 7UXiI2arAlQ ~0:34, L40); the drawing rule is ("mark out the body of the 9:29 a.m. candle, just like this. Use a rectangle, and then this is going to be the fair price for the day." — JJ, 7UXiI2arAlQ ~1:10, L58) and ("no need to include the wicks." — JJ, 7UXiI2arAlQ ~6:04, L205). The same body rule appears in April (("I'm going to mark out the pre-open candle 9:29 just the body of that candle right here." — JJ, Lep139B5EbU ~2:34, L117)) and June (("First, fair price that I will assume is just 9:29 Eastern before the market opens. And then from there, if there's consolidation and then more breakouts, I'll just read the most recent consolidation as a fair price." — JJ, aCOgfvL6lK8 ~17:48, L672)). In other videos the same reference is called the opening price: ("I believe the 9:30 a.m. is the fair price. Any move away from that is unfair. So, I would trade a reversion back to the opening price." — JJ, 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~10:07, L366); ("The fair price will be the market opening 95% of the time" — JJ, KN7j6NXXAio ~2:45, L137); ("coming into the session, this is going to be my fair price, just the opening price of the 9:30 a.m. session." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~3:52, L190); and, with the news-day qualifier, ("On non-news days, the open is going to be fair most of the time, like 95% of the time." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:13:12, L2310). He says the two readings are interchangeable (medium confidence; the caption is garbled): ("Opening candle at 9:30 right here. The opening price seems quite fair. Well, you can also dial half of 929. It's more or less the same thing." — JJ, CzxSgYujDxs ~22:59, L762). The general form is ("fair price is basically the price before the session open, because when the session opens, there's an influx of volume from institutions that are willing to trade unfairly." — JJ, UVKVSWKFlvo ~0:22, L41). Two backtesters read it the same way: the FX Replay host says ("somebody said they thought it was the 9:29 candle. It's basically market open." — host, SNO1wqJTq5A ~7:57, L324) and the Vai host uses the whole candle rather than the body (("He typically just does the body, but I'm just going to do the whole candle" — host, c61c4CxTpYI ~2:00, L87)).

**Reference 2: the pre-open consolidation, when the open runs straight through it.** The 9:29 print is not sacred: ("Just understand that 9:29 is not always going to be the fair price because there can be unfair moves pre-market open." — JJ, 8lbkj0PF1uM ~9:00, L305). Test: if the open is in the middle of the overnight range it is fair (("it looks like it's opening in the middle of the overnight range. So, it is probably opening at a fair price. I'll just mark out the open." — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~9:12, L270)); if the opening move goes straight to a pre-open consolidation and beyond, that consolidation is fair and the open is not (("this is the chunk of consolidation right before the market open. Now, I know market opened down here. But when it opened, it went straight up to this consolidation and then even farther beyond. So, open not likely to be fair." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:19:50, L2513)). A pre-open volume spike moves the reference to the candle before the spike: ("Usually at at 25 after there is sometimes a volume spike which creates an unfair move. So instead of having the open price is fair. I'm looking more towards that uh 24 after candle" — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:30:38, L2843); ("fair price could be this consolidation right here before the initial volume spike which technically did happen 6 minutes pre-open." — JJ, PN1UKQMPb5M ~19:52, L622); ("it was only going to trade back towards this as the fair price because it's the consolidation before that pre-market volume spike." — JJ, g68HHbrIBBg ~2:37, L124). Also JJ, MIWsdldzJgQ ~4:00, L163 (overnight consolidation as fair; the open went straight to it) and PN1UKQMPb5M ~5:55, L206.

**Reference 3: the pre-news price on scheduled-news days.** ("the fair price of news is going to be the price before the news came out. This is because this news is planned. If the news is planned, they already have a forecast for it." — JJ, 8lbkj0PF1uM ~1:44, L84); the drawing rule is the 8:29 candle body (("mark out the candle before news. So, 8:30 a.m. was news. And I'll mark out here the body of the candle before the news. So, this would be 8:29 a.m. EST." — JJ, 8lbkj0PF1uM ~3:37, L141)), because ("Most of the time it's pretty much exactly on the forecast." — JJ, 8lbkj0PF1uM ~1:54, L89). On such days the 9:30 open is explicitly the wrong anchor: ("if you mark this out and say this is my fair price cuz it's 930, unfortunately, you're going to be wrong. And the reason for this is because when the market opened, the volume came in and it's going to move to a fair price, which is the pre-news price." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:03:48, L2023). The choice between the two is made by the first minutes after the open: ("So I would no longer consider morning to be fair after 9:34. I would only look to trade back towards the pre-news price." — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~14:09, L496); ("worst case scenario, the open is fair. Like, the lower fair price would be the open because it's only been going down. So the fair price is either the open or the pre-news price, right?" — JJ, g68HHbrIBBg ~13:27, L513); ("I stopped looking at the open because if the open were fair, this green candle would have printed as a huge red candle and we would have been back to the open by now." — JJ, g68HHbrIBBg ~14:57, L567). The same logic covers CPI and FOMC: ("CPI obviously today CPI is right here. That is the fair price." — JJ, HlWSP7ajgpQ ~14:34, L491); ("the pre-FOMC price is extremely fair just because it's already priced in what the rate is going to be if it's changing or not." — JJ, xTxrtFKlQfc ~5:56, L227); and the reversions on a news morning target the 8:30 print (("looking for reversions to this red line, which is the open of the 8:30 a.m. news event" — JJ, l6iq0ljhxIo ~13:02, L444)). The September summary: ("We're going to assume a specific fair price in the market, which would be a session open or a pre-news event." — JJ, DEkzU8gqX-o ~0:51, L59).

**VWAP.** Acceptable as an average estimate, rejected for news: ("You could use VWAP. It is a pretty good estimate on average." — JJ, DEkzU8gqX-o ~7:35, L287); ("the only problem is VWAP doesn't really accurately account for news. So, that's why I personally don't use it." — JJ, DEkzU8gqX-o ~7:47, L294). No other fact in the corpus mentions VWAP.

### From the primary corpus: when fair price moves and when it stops being a target

**Reference 4: the most recent consolidation, once price has left the anchor on volume.** ("the most recent consolidation is usually what I would term to be the fair price." — JJ, RnwOqmEF6Rk ~2:25, L124); ("I change fair price throughout the day based on most recent consolidation." — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~2:34, L94). The trigger is consolidation, then a break of structure, then continuation away from the old anchor: ("I believe fair price to now be this area right here because we consolidated then broke structure and continued away." — JJ, HlWSP7ajgpQ ~2:39, L114); ("I would delete the fair price at 9:30. Now, I'm going to assume that the fair price is this most recent consolidation, because when the volume came in, it moved away from the session opening" — JJ, KN7j6NXXAio ~11:00, L434); ("I would adjust the fair price from the open to right here before this candle spawned in at 9:51. Because when the candle spawned in, a bunch of volume just took it up farther." — JJ, EyR3i2GxwyA ~6:32, L245). The logical test he states repeatedly: ("If the open was fair, this move up here would have been a move down. It would have came straight back down to the open, but it didn't. So I would now treat this as fair." — JJ, l6iq0ljhxIo ~5:31, L205). The new zone is drawn as top, bottom and middle (("So just marked the hop in the bottom of the consolidation. There's the middle of it." — JJ, l6iq0ljhxIo ~5:12, L195)). Three qualifiers: the move away must be large enough (("It never made enough of a move away for me to adjust fair price to here. So, I'm taking a pretty long trade back up to market open." — JJ, PN1UKQMPb5M ~24:04, L747)); the consolidation must carry volume (("all this consolidation here was unfair basically because there is no volume." — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~3:57, L160); ("consolidation helps to indicate a fair price if it is during a session, because that is when the volume is most obvious" — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~23:14, L795)); and the direction of the peak-volume break decides which side is fair (("when the peak volume of these candles arrived, it dropped. If it had gone back up, then obviously I would have bought when it broke back down to here, but it broke down. So, I would treat this most recent consolidation as a fair price and simply buy there." — JJ, CzxSgYujDxs ~22:37, L750)). One duration datum: after a news spike he waited for ("There's the 30 minutes of consolidation. This is me marking out a fair price right here." — JJ, RnwOqmEF6Rk ~2:29, L126). Losing reversions are themselves a signal to move the anchor: ("I try to adapt fair price if my trades are losing. So, I won't keep trading back towards the market open if it's trending heavily in one direction." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~32:36, L1069). He concedes this layer is not mechanical: ("It is a little bit discretionary. Unfortunately, I can't make it 100% mechanical exactly how to do it." — JJ, l6iq0ljhxIo ~4:36, L176); ("I just rely on a little bit of discretion to identify fair value." — JJ, DEkzU8gqX-o ~7:47, L294).

**The first-move test for a new session open (6 p.m. versus 8 p.m.).** The rule is the same as reference 4 but applied at the Asian open: ("is 8:00 p.m. fair or is 6 p.m. fair? If you said 6 p.m., that is correct because it opened. Huge move down. The first move was down. Uh, so 6 p.m. is fair. I am not going to revert to Asian. I'm going to revert to six." — JJ, BLvsYJ4sqn8 ~13:51, L459); (medium) ("I switched fair price from six to Asian because Asian opened down. If Asian opens up, then I would leave fair price at six." — JJ, BLvsYJ4sqn8 ~7:12, L254); ("8 p.m. open is a red candle, but it didn't do anything. There's no movement short. So, I would still be considering 6 p.m. is fair." — JJ, BLvsYJ4sqn8 ~26:01, L834); ("when Asian opened, boom, it went away from 6 PM. So 6 PM down here is no longer fair. Asian open is fair. Just trade a reversion." — JJ, l6iq0ljhxIo ~2:22, L105); ("since the first move was bullish, I no longer see 6:00 p.m. as a target." — JJ, g68HHbrIBBg ~8:51, L348). The same test applied at 9:30 against the overnight anchor: ("Fair price likely is the open because it opened up away from the Asian price so I can delete that." — JJ, EyR3i2GxwyA ~2:24, L109). In words: an open that moves away from the prior anchor becomes the new anchor; an open whose first move is back toward the prior anchor confirms the prior anchor; an open that does nothing leaves the prior anchor in place.

**Two or three candidates.** When the test is inconclusive he takes the midpoint and trades between them: ("I just put a line in the middle of all of them and then treated it as the fair price for most of the session." — JJ, CzxSgYujDxs ~4:37, L177); ("So, it's either here or here. So, I put it in the middle of them. Uh because my whole goal here is to have a bias." — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~20:09, L560); ("worst case, you can just trade to and from them with a 1.5 risk to reward." — JJ, EyR3i2GxwyA ~12:28, L441); ("Just trading from and towards the 6:00 p.m. and Asian session because one of them is right." — JJ, g68HHbrIBBg ~17:11, L647); when 6 p.m. and 8 p.m. coincide, ("this area right here between these two lines is the most fair price ever." — JJ, GMDUiamqgig ~6:26, L221). When unsure he picks the direction of the next likely fair value: ("Where's the next most likely fair value? Well, the long direction because the reversion would be reverting these shorts." — JJ, l6iq0ljhxIo ~19:44, L657).

**Unexpected news resets the anchor.** ("Since the news was unexpected, it will change the fair market price." — JJ, 4IGbxmKJ4BU ~6:56, L325); ("instead of trading back to the pre-news price, because the move is fair, I would treat the most recent consolidation after the news to be the new fair price" — JJ, 8lbkj0PF1uM ~10:05, L338); ("unexpected news, this is a new fair price and I would just expect a slight positive drift if the news was positive." — JJ, 8lbkj0PF1uM ~2:55, L120). A large miss on scheduled news is treated as unexpected: ("Wednesday, pretty far off, so um in this case it's sort of unexpected in a way. Then, I'll just treat this consolidation and the 9:29 a.m. candle as the fair price." — JJ, 8lbkj0PF1uM ~6:42, L235); a small beat shifts the target to the post-news consolidation (("instead of going down to the pre-news price, I was just looking at this value here because the news was a little better than expected for the Nasdaq." — JJ, g68HHbrIBBg ~18:21, L689)). Unscheduled shocks (tweets) are recognised from the tape: ("the volume increased out of nowhere at a random time and then it just started consolidating after. So, this is a new fair price." — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~29:51, L817). Retracted news is ignored: ("Fake news is very weird when you're doing fair pricing theory. Um, basically just pretend it didn't happen." — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~39:17, L1067). The anchor also drifts with the sign of the day's inputs: ("from here over time fair price changes right and I feel like it's going to change in the positive direction because news was green and open was green" — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~42:54, L1163).

**When the reference stops being a target.** After 11:00 the 9:30 price is demoted from target to bias: ("9:30 is not a target after 11:00 a.m." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~41:28, L1340); ("It is just a high time frame bias for reversion because in the long run markets are reverting." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~41:32, L1342). Midday has no volume to revert on: ("Between 11 to 2 p.m. there is basically no volume. It's kind of a dead time in the market. So, it is possible, slightly possible for price to be trading at an unfair price between 11 and 2 p.m." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~42:39, L1376); ("during the hours of 11:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m., the volume is dead and it's unable to return to its fair price." — JJ, 7UXiI2arAlQ ~2:34, L100). Price too close to the anchor is not traded: ("it's too close to take reversals at 6:00 p.m. Because remember, it's 6:00 p.m. It's a fair price." — JJ, g68HHbrIBBg ~1:07, L70); a whole evening was skipped because ("This one literally did not move more than 20 points away from anything." — JJ, l6iq0ljhxIo ~10:40, L369). Trading away from the anchor is the defined error: ("the fair price is the price prior to the opening right here. And if you go short here, that means you're trading away from it." — JJ, J8qXxtFFpGg ~10:35, L442). The 6 p.m. open is fair ("probably 98% of the time" — JJ, l6iq0ljhxIo ~18:59, L633) but not always, because ("there's not enough volume transacted between 5 and 6 for that hour when the market's in its downtime for it to open 207 points higher than where it is." — JJ, l6iq0ljhxIo ~19:20, L644). The distance to the anchor is the expected payoff and drives account selection: ("if you think the fair price is here, you know you'll get 80 points. You know you'll get 50 points. You know you'll get 100 points, and then you know exactly how many points you're going to get." — JJ, q8PXXvY9lS0 ~5:45, L221).

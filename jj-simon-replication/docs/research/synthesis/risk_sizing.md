### From the primary corpus: stops and targets

**1. Rules in his own words**

*1.1 Static distances, never structure.* The stop and the target are fixed point or dollar distances chosen for the account's rule set, not chart levels: ("Each stop loss I use is static. I'm not going to put my stop loss at the previous low or above the previous high because that's what many strategies do. But that's not what the drawdown of a funding account does" — JJ, PMGhHCjYG5c ~17:08, L724); ("I just target a specific amount of points because that's how I backtested." — JJ, RnwOqmEF6Rk ~3:25, L157); ("on prop firms, it is infinitely better to use static risk in terms of like exactly 1,000, exactly 500." — JJ, -lxNWJGWtbg ~7:19, L306); ("You should absolutely not have a discretionary stop loss. Each one of them should be fixed. You must link it to the mathematics of the evaluation according to your strategy and success rate" — JJ, J8qXxtFFpGg ~6:44, L291). What varies is the contract count: ("And with the stop-loss, I only change the contract size. My take profit and stop-loss are static." — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~13:27, L473). "Risk management" is the triple: ("whenever I say risk management, I will mean your stop-loss, your profit target, and your contract sizing." — JJ, sWJa8vRfPb8 ~2:54, L127). One target, no scaling out: ("Leaving runners and partials is probably one of the worst things you can do on prop firms." — JJ, aCOgfvL6lK8 ~6:46, L300).

*1.2 The evaluation bracket: 25-point stop, 38-point target.* ("I'm just going to have all of them be 38 points TP, 25 points stop loss, and then you can choose your contract sizing based on your risk aversion." — JJ, 3L8xdh3oPm4 ~12:56, L459); ("So I'll just use a 3825. That's what my programmed backtest has indicated as the most cost-effective for a 1 to 1.5 risk-reward ratio." — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~2:28, L111); ("I usually, or actually always, use 3825 for my valuation operations. It's a 1:1.5, and it's the best way to pass the evaluations of funding companies." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~1:13, L70); ("Evaluations always going to be a 1 to 1.5. It's going to be 2538 or change the contract size to make it 5076." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~9:37, L366). Captions write the pair as "3825", "2538", "38.25" or "25.38"; all mean 38 target over 25 stop. In dollars on one NQ contract: ("So if I'm doing 25 and 38 with one contract of NQ features, this is -500 plus 760." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:07:52, L2147). The 76 (not 75) in the doubled bracket is a commission allowance: ("I do 76 instead of 75 because there are commissions and fees. So that basically makes it a 75." — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~15:35, L543). The 25-point stop is also the modal output of his optimiser: ("the most common stop losses that I end up seeing from my optimization is 25 points." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:00:56, L1935), with the alternatives ("37.5, 50, and 75. So, a few different options, but again, you have to optimize it for your specific account." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:01:04, L1939). Floor in the morning session: ("Never less than 25 points in the New York session." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:11:55, L2271). Session dependence: ("But I usually do 25 points stop especially on the less volatile sessions. New York sometimes I'll do 50 but for these less volatile ones it's easier to do 25." — JJ, EyR3i2GxwyA ~2:00, L96).

*1.3 The opening-candle rule: over 25 points, double the bracket and halve the contracts.* This is the only volatility adjustment he states in his own words, and he states it in at least ten videos. ("The candle is 29 points, so I would do a 50 point stop and a 76 point take profit. My usual rule for that is if the candle's over 25 points, then I don't do my 25 stop 38 profit. I would divide size by two and I'll do 50 and 76." — JJ, UVKVSWKFlvo ~1:56, L93); ("If the opening candle is larger than 25 points I'll just simplify it. Cut my contracts in half. That would make this 76 and that would make this 50." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~4:41, L215); ("if the opening candle is over 25 points, I'll use a 5076 instead of a 2538. So that's a small ATR adjustment you can change." — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~13:18, L468); ("opening candle, 23.5 points. Remember, 25 is our benchmark that determines when we will use more or less volume." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~25:09, L862). What is measured is the candle body, in the two videos where he says which: ("The body of the opening candle is 18 points, so risk management says, "Go with a 25-point stop, 38-point take profit."" — JJ, UVKVSWKFlvo ~6:55, L258); ("The candle body is 33 points, which means I will size my contracts in half, which gives me a 50-point stop and a 76-point take profit" — JJ, xTxrtFKlQfc ~1:57, L94). The boundary is inclusive in one video (("50 point stop loss because the candle was 25 points or more and a 76 point take profit for my standard 1 to 1.5 risk to reward trade." — JJ, EyR3i2GxwyA ~0:44, L54)) and a 26-point candle still qualifies (("It is 26 points. So I would still do the 0.5." — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~18:59, L529)). The rule is per session, not per day: the 18:00 Asian opening candle triggers it too (("Asia opened with the biggest red candle I've ever seen, 32 points, was go short with a 5075-76." — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~15:35, L543); ("Opening candle was 40 points, so that means I will use 50 point stop, 76 point take profit." — JJ, EyR3i2GxwyA ~1:22, L75)). It applies to the continuation off that candle; in the backtest video reversions keep one contract: ("I'll only do the half contract on opening opening one." — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~2:07, L82). He treats the two brackets as equivalent in expectation: ("Sometimes I'll do 76-50. It's more or less the same." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~38:25, L1301); ("stop losses don't really matter on prop firms. It's all the same EV. The profitari is where the EV is generated or lost." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:28:52, L2789) (the caption's "profitari" is "profit target").

*1.4 Wider stops on reversions with a lot of room or volume (discretionary).* ("Now I know I usually say 2538. There's a little discretion you can add. In this case you have 243 points in your favor. If it's over 100, then I would probably give in and place the stop at 50 points just because the volume is extremely high that day." — JJ, CzxSgYujDxs ~9:05, L319; medium confidence); ("in this one, I did use a 50-point stop loss just because there was 150 points in my favor, so I sized down on this trade." — JJ, xTxrtFKlQfc ~4:01, L163); ("This one was actually 50 point stop on both of these trades just because of how much volume there was. Again, 157 point candle." — JJ, xTxrtFKlQfc ~6:05, L232). Entry location matters: ("if I enter at the top of these then I want to have a larger stop loss because I assume some part of retracement is normal. If I'm entering down here it's fine to have a lower smaller stop loss." — JJ, PN1UKQMPb5M ~24:51, L770).

*1.5 No management, with named exceptions.* ("I never go break even, which might be interesting to hear, unless there is a new session opening. Like, if I'm in a trade at 6:00 p.m. and then Asian session opens, I will go break even." — JJ, -lxNWJGWtbg ~8:50, L358); ("Or, if we have news coming out, then I will go break even. But, like 95% of my trades I'm not going to go break even." — JJ, -lxNWJGWtbg ~8:57, L362); ("I do recommend going break even if the market's going to open while you're in a trade, just because you have crazy wicks like this, just filled with the overnight volume." — JJ, UVKVSWKFlvo ~4:41, L184). A rarer third exception is a structure break against a stalled continuation: ("I'm not usually a fan of going to the break-even point unless it's absolutely necessary. In this case, it is. The reason for that is that the initial unfair continuation basically stops and breaks the structure against you" — JJ, KN7j6NXXAio ~16:39, L637); ("I set this stop to break even just because if it breaks structure down, then it's probably cooked." — JJ, PN1UKQMPb5M ~13:36, L435). The 50%-to-target break-even rule in the "40 Trades" backtest is the host's own: ("At this point, I would move my stops to break even, meaning I have accomplished half of the space here. So, I do tend to move my stops break even that way." — host, c61c4CxTpYI ~9:08, L240).

- What to do when the candle is big: ("In the opening candle, if it is greater than 25 points, you can reduce the size, but 3825 will be fine." — JJ, 0uAQUHEB_L8 ~17:02, L639) against ("then I don't do my 25 stop 38 profit. The reward is where everything changes." — JJ, AxP-cg50TdM ~6:56, L259) against ("I've lost far more accounts due to poor sizing than due to bad operations." — JJ, PMGhHCjYG5c ~14:02, L598) and ("And when you're in a trade with a clear trend, the higher your stop loss, the more likely you are to profit from it" — JJ, S8fSNbPFuLA ~7:33, L322).

### From the primary corpus: contract sizing, room to fair value and account size

**1. Rules in his own words**

*1.1 Dollars are the invariant; contracts are the dial.* ("The same dollar amount is the most important thing to do on prop firms because it's optimal." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:08:39, L2171); ("now you have a 50 point stop loss and a 76 point take profit. But what you should do is you cut your contract in half." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:08:19, L2161); ("just make like the exact same point stop-loss profit target in points and we would just manipulate your contracts based on what site you're trading with" — JJ, r5AhmGaFujU ~18:58, L594); ("The size of the position should not be subjective. And by position size I mean the contracts and how big your take profit and stop loss are. There must be a statistically optimal size for your take profit, your contracts, and your stop loss in dollar terms." — JJ, J8qXxtFFpGg ~6:17, L274). Contracts also follow room to fair value: ("So, you can change the amount of contracts you use based on how many points to the fair price. It is something I do." — JJ, MVP7X-3v8xk ~2:57, L104).

*1.2 The half bracket (12.5 / 19) and the minimum room to fair value.* When fewer than about 30 points separate the entry from fair value he halves the bracket and doubles contracts so the dollar outcome is unchanged: ("if there are 30 to 76 points in my favor for the evaluations, then I will normally only have a profit take of 38 points. If there are 15 to 30 points in my favor for the evaluation, then I will do this one for 19 points." — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~9:59, L360); ("38 divided by two gives you 19. And then 25 for your stop loss divided by two gives you 12.5. So, you could take a trade of 38 and 25, double the contracts, now it's 19 and 12.5, and it's still the same amount of dollars at risk" — JJ, 74CRg-mID5c ~9:30, L344); ("remember, I usually do 38 25. But if there aren't 38 points in my favor, in this case, there were only 15, I can do 19 12.5" — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~20:32, L706); ("So maybe I'd go for 19 points, which is half of 38, but double the number of contracts I take because the most important thing is to keep my static profit target the same." — JJ, KhooqEQK9bA ~6:46, L300). The same scaling runs upward: ("would be appropriate for you to do minus 100 points, plus 152 points. That's just one example. Uh, but if it's 170, sometimes you can add, you can use this kind of scaling mechanism that I drew here." — JJ, 7KJOh2NF-lQ ~22:17, L764). Room thresholds for the standard target: ("just for this video, I'm doing the 38 point wins, which means I need 30 points." — JJ, BLvsYJ4sqn8 ~11:19, L381); ("you can't even trade this when it's 14 points away. You could trade this and lose. Uh, where is six? Six is here. We need it to be 30 points away." — JJ, BLvsYJ4sqn8 ~26:56, L862); ("And it's giving 33 and 1/2 points, so target 38 points." — JJ, UVKVSWKFlvo ~8:40, L316). For larger targets the rule is proportional: ("if I want to target 100 point win, then I need at least 80 points in my favor to take that trade. If I only have like 40 points to fair price, there's no way I should let a trade run for 100. 80% 75 if I'm getting a little bit greedy is sort of the mark" — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:20:59, L2548); between two account targets he takes the lower: ("So 50 would probably be a little bit better than a 75 point target in this example, but nonetheless very good for an evaluation giving 3825." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:07:05, L2123).

*1.3 50K versus 150K.* ("In this case, I doubled the stop loss and take profit because I was on a 150k account. What I talk about usually is 25 point stop 38 point take profit. That's good for 50k accounts." — JJ, Lep139B5EbU ~2:47, L124); ("So, again same thing 50 point stop, 75-ish point take profit uh on my 150k accounts. Just like very standard for me, and I'll change the amount of contract based on how confident I am in my position." — JJ, Lep139B5EbU ~5:46, L221). It's not going to work on 150K. Now you have way more drawdown. So you might have to even do like minus 2K, plus 4K, 6K, whatever." — JJ, C-_0QhKZmA4 ~13:20, L468); ("if you have a very good strategy for 30, 40, 50 point trades, and you suddenly move to 150K, that won't generate as much expected value as it would in the 50K trade. So you would need a strategy that takes longer trades." — JJ, 4BXpI-hYqe0 ~8:23, L317). Two 50Ks can stand in for a 150K: ("Basically, you're like replicating a 150K account, but with two 50K accounts. You can also just do half contract size on both of them, and then both 2K's would look like the same thing." — JJ, C-_0QhKZmA4 ~4:14, L165). On evaluations the money at risk is the fee, so exposure is added on the 50Ks: ("If you're on a 150K eval, it's like 300 bucks. The 50K is 100 bucks. So 350s equal 150 in terms of your actual risk like money at risk." — JJ, VY-bO35-tjE ~17:42, L695) (the caption's "350s" is "three 50s"); ("So start sending 150Ks and then layer in 50Ks. That can also help reduce the risk." — JJ, VY-bO35-tjE ~18:11, L713). The 150K target drops to 38 when fair value is close: ("Uh this one was 38 point take profit. I know I said I usually do 50 and 75. Um that's like I don't know. In general for my 150k accounts, but there's no point in me doing 50 75 here when I believe the fair price is like right here." — JJ, Lep139B5EbU ~9:44, L351).

*1.4 Contracts per account.* ("The reason is that in a 50K account you get two contracts and in a 150K account you get a maximum of three contracts" — JJ, 4BXpI-hYqe0 ~7:43, L294); ("I lost 50, lost 50, then won 250. Uh because I got to get in and out pretty quickly." — JJ, PN1UKQMPb5M ~21:53, L682); ("I'm trying to use max 15 or 20 MNQ and I enter with the absolute max 15 NQ and the trade just loses like 10 grand in a matter of few minutes on the open" — JJ, dJdBnSBJlgQ ~13:00, L399); ("Eight contracts is crazy. Do we teach that in mentoring? I would" — JJ, MIWsdldzJgQ ~14:26, L504; medium). Contract count is derived from the account's dollar target and the points available: ("I have $6,000 profit target and with 50 points that would be six contracts. So that's the reason for that. Just trading a one to one here." — JJ, KHEQ5g55dQ4 ~1:33:42, L2937); ("The size is solely for the risk management of my account. I need a profit of 7.5k. Or "I'm not too confident about this one. I'll just use one contract maybe get a little bit of money." That's That's also not good. You need to have a standardized system." — JJ, 2hwd27aqD30 ~2:18, L113).

*1.5 The ATR ladder is the fxreplay host's.* The 50 / 25 / 16.5 ladder keyed to an ATR above 20, 7-20 and below 7, with 1 / 2 / 3 contracts for about $1,000, is described by the presenter as his own testing: ("if it's over 20, like, price is really moving, um, 50 tick stop loss is going to be a good, um, stop loss distance.

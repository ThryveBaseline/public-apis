# What makes it work, in his words

A mechanism-first reading of the primary corpus (`docs/research/EXTRACTED.md`): not whether his claims are true, but what each part of the method is for and why the parts fit. Quotes are verbatim, cited by video id, approximate time and transcript line.

## 1. The market edge: an unfair move, then a return to the price before it

The core claim is that a session open or a news release injects volume that moves price away from where it was fairly priced, and that within the session that move tends to come back. "here's the opening of the New York session. I'm going to treat that as what I consider a fair price. I'll take a sequel and then just look for reversals." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~16:45, L629) "the strategy I operate will utilize a continuation of the session opening, which means capitalizing on an unfair move with a volume entry, and then taking multiple reversals towards my personal bias." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~16:08, L607) The first trade in a session goes with the opening push (continuation); after that he fades the move back toward the open (reversion), re-entering each time price moves away again: "So, there is a continuation, a reversal. After that, it's too close to reverse here. You need to wait for another downward move, which we would get here, and then I saw another entry by offset." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~17:24, L652) "every time it moves away, you get a nice green candle coming back towards it. Basically, getting a lot of volume on a day without much volume." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~20:32, L764) The signals are binary and structural: "There is no structural break, so there are no continuations until the first one we get here." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~16:57, L636) "then I saw another entry by offset. It simply means that the candle is larger than the previous one and closes above it." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~17:32, L657)

What this buys him is frequency with a small per-trade edge: "The goal here is to have biased inputs. Because that will give you a slight advantage in every transaction you make. When you make 10 to 20 entries daily, that small advantage adds up to a lot." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~16:18, L613) and because every session open creates the same situation, the method repeats across the day: "But my strategy works for all sessions. New York, 2:00 p.m., 8:30 a.m. If there is news. It works for the Asian session, the London session, the 6:00 p.m. opening, all sessions." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~15:39, L590)

## 2. The bracket: a fixed bet with a known distribution

A fixed stop and target turn the setup into a repeatable bet whose win rate can be measured: "And remember, I always use 3825. Sometimes I'll reduce the size, sometimes I'll increase it, but you can always use 3825 and it will be the same. In the opening candle, if it is greater than 25 points, you can reduce the size, but 3825 will be fine." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~17:02, L639) "For example, if I could put this at 50, this would be 75. So there are infinite possibilities of a 1.5 risk-reward ratio. I could even reduce the size, but what would that mean? 12.5 and then this would be 19." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~20:02, L746) He is explicit that the realistic win rate at that bracket is modest: "Remember that it's a 1.5. On average, you'll win about 41% of the time, realistically speaking." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~18:22, L687) At 1.5R the break-even win rate is 40%, so the bet is positive but thin; the method does not rely on a large per-trade edge, it relies on the next two layers.

## 3. The prop-firm layer: where the money is made

He says the strategy itself is not the point on a prop firm: "stop trading prop firms like a live account. If you're trading on a live account, your only goal is to make money. That is not your goal with a prop firm. With a prop firm, your goal is to have this payout be bigger than this, expenses." (XhsbfEdJBAc ~8:57, L339) The evaluation is a capped-risk bet on a thin edge, so it is attacked hard: "Rule two, once you have your model, attack the evaluations aggressively. An eval is not a funded account. The most important thing to understand is that your risk is completely capped. You basically have no downside." (RGYbTD1j628 ~1:15, L72) and the only statistic that matters there is the pass rate: "the eval, all you need to optimize for is your pass rate. I don't care what your win rate is, I don't care your risk to reward. All I care is how many times you hit plus 3K before minus 2K. That is your entire goal in this simulated account." (XhsbfEdJBAc ~6:57, L270) The funded account is a different bet, optimised for payout rate times payout size: "the eval's whole goal is to pass, the funded's whole goal is to generate total payout. Basically, it just multiplies these two together. So, 30% of a $2,000 payout, increase the payout size or increase your percentage." (XhsbfEdJBAc ~7:53, L302) so he trades it differently from the evaluation: "So, I have optimized the strategy where I'm really aggressive on the evals just to pass them super quick. That way I can decrease the months to payout, which obviously gives me more money per month." (XhsbfEdJBAc ~12:13, L452) and lowers risk as the balance grows: "I hope you understand why I would reduce my risk as my funded accounts increase their balance. Obviously, it's entirely up to you, but when the expected value is exactly the same, if you're a trader operating in equilibrium, if the expected value is equal, your goal should be to reduce variance as much as possible." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~11:54, L456) Firms are chosen by the arithmetic of their rules: "if you're trading prop firm challenges, you want to buy the cheapest ones as possible if you have an edge because you want your risk of ruin to reduce as much as possible so that you're able to realize more of your edge in the long run." (aBVn6IUAjnM ~2:31, L102)

## 4. Variance control: many accounts, one trade each, hard daily limits

Frequency lets him spread trades across accounts instead of copying one trade to all of them, which keeps expected value and cuts variance: "I don't even need to copy trade. If I have 30 accounts and I take 30 trades per day, that's one trade on every single account." (XhsbfEdJBAc ~5:23, L216) "And if you're able to take one trade on every single account, you're probably never going to tilt and you're able to decrease variance, but keep the exact same expected value across all of your trades instead of copy trading." (XhsbfEdJBAc ~5:28, L219) Each account has a daily profit target and a hard daily stop: "Now, onto rule four. Every single account gets a daily profit target and a hard daily loss limit. Without these, you'll either overtrade your good days or avenge trade your bad days." (RGYbTD1j628 ~2:34, L124) "Hit the profit target, walk away. Hit the stop loss, also walk away. Come back tomorrow, no more trading." (RGYbTD1j628 ~3:01, L142) And he treats the evaluation fee, not the account, as the unit of risk: "all these things will slowly increase to the point where your side of the inequality is larger than the prop firm side of the eval fee. Eventually, you're more profitable than the prop firm is in that sense" (aBVn6IUAjnM ~25:52, L851)

## 5. Why the parts fit

* A reversion edge that recurs at every session open gives many small, measurable bets a day.
* A fixed 1.5R bracket makes the pass rate on a 50k rule set (+$3,000 before -$2,000) something you can backtest, and that pass rate is the whole business.
* Capped-risk evaluations bought cheaply and attacked aggressively convert a thin trade edge into funded accounts at a known cost per account.
* Many funded accounts traded one trade a day each, with daily limits, convert the thin edge into a payout stream whose variance is low enough to survive on.
* The firm's rules (consistency, trailing drawdown, payout caps, five accounts per firm) set the risk per account and the number of firms; the operation is sized to the rules, not the other way round.

The replication code mirrors these layers: `fpt/strategy.py` (layers 1-2), `fpt/risk.py` and the `evaluation` command (pass rate under the firm's rule), `fpt/propfirm.py` and `portfolio` (layers 3-4 at scale), `fpt/bootstrap.py` and `growth` (the same from a small bankroll, see `docs/BOOTSTRAP.md`).

# Starting small: the bootstrap path

How to get from a few hundred dollars to a multi-account operation using payouts only, built from his own statements (every quote below is verbatim from the transcript corpus, cited by video id, approximate time and transcript line; see `docs/research/EXTRACTED.md`) and from the two growth simulators in `fpt/bootstrap.py`.

## 1. His own framing of the small-bankroll problem

He started with a large bankroll himself: "I had a large bankroll like 25k to start with and prop firm evals back then were pretty cheap, like $50. That's probably a bit more now." (aBVn6IUAjnM ~23:33, L777). But he addresses the $500 case directly: "how much am I going to spend on my first prop firm challenge if I have $500 to invest? Or which prop firm am I going to try? Which risk reward am I going to try?" (aBVn6IUAjnM ~4:42, L172) His answer is bankroll management from poker. First, buy the cheapest challenges: "if you're trading prop firm challenges, you want to buy the cheapest ones as possible if you have an edge because you want your risk of ruin to reduce as much as possible so that you're able to realize more of your edge in the long run." (aBVn6IUAjnM ~2:31, L102) Second, size the payout target to the bankroll: "Like if you're targeting higher risk, higher reward, then you're going to need a larger bankroll, obviously, because it you're you're less likely to hit your your massive payout in that sense. So, small bankroll, you want to target smaller payouts until you're able to build the bankroll." (aBVn6IUAjnM ~4:55, L179) Third, no copy trading while small: "Also, do not copy trade. Copy trading is going to increase your variance like crazy. There's no reason you should ever be copy trading if you have less than $10,000 to invest in prop firms, because that is going to increase your risk of ruin so significantly" (wsKx7L9EJys ~8:09, L275) and "So, don't don't copy trade if you don't have to. Ideally, find a strategy that works multiple times per day so you're able to trade multiple accounts." (aBVn6IUAjnM ~8:58, L309)

The statistics to find before spending anything: "You really want to know what your pass rate is, what your average payout is, what your average payout chance is. Obviously, you can't know these things when you're just starting, but uh if you're going to invest $500 to $1,000 in your trading, you really want to find out some of these statistics" (aBVn6IUAjnM ~13:58, L469) On the evaluation the only statistic that matters is the pass rate, defined on a 50k account as reaching +$3,000 before -$2,000: "The only required edge is a 33% pass rate on the evals, meaning a 33% chance to reach plus 3K before minus 2K. This is the only stat to backtest as well." (wsKx7L9EJys ~1:54, L82) "Do not bother backtesting your live equity curve. That's not going to matter because we're trading on prop firms. All we need to know is the hit rate of the profit target before the max drawdown. So, how many times you can win before lose on the eval. That's all you need to backtest for." (wsKx7L9EJys ~2:00, L85) His own backtest is exactly that simulation: "it simulates that you have an account with a funding firm. It starts with a balance of 50,000 dollars, and if you ever lose 2,000 dollars, it means you've reached the maximum loss and your account is lost. See how many times you can reach 53,000 before dropping 2,000." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~2:30, L121)

## 2. His worked example: $500 to $13,500

The "$10,000 a month" video (wsKx7L9EJys, 2026-05-10) is the bootstrap recipe. Start on one firm: "I'll tell you the exact five firms I run this on later to scale, but for now, Lusis going to be enough." (wsKx7L9EJys ~2:41, L106) (captions garble "Lucid is"). Evaluation risk: "You can risk about 500 to $2,000 per trade, profit target about 1,000 to 1,500 per trade." (wsKx7L9EJys ~1:02, L55) Buy and reset until funded: "Step one, buy in 10 evaluations and turn that into 10 funded accounts. If you have a this 33% pass rate, that means it's going to take on average three attempts to pass each evaluation." (wsKx7L9EJys ~2:22, L96) "10 evals with three attempts means 30 total attempts. If it's about $100 each, that's $3,000 in total expenses. You can reset your failed evals until they pass. So, your end state is 10 funded accounts." (wsKx7L9EJys ~2:31, L101) The cycle and its compounding: "Step eight, realize the compounding path. This 33% pass rate produces about 3x per cycle because we invested 3K and we took out 9K. So, if you have $500, one cycle of this can become 1,500, which then becomes 4,500, which then becomes 13.5K" (wsKx7L9EJys ~7:12, L246) Discipline that makes the arithmetic hold: "You need to understand to realize expected value, you cannot tilt. You have to place stick to one trade per day on each account, and do not tilt. If you lose going for a winning day, no matter what, wait until the next day to take another trade. It is more expected value if you wait." (wsKx7L9EJys ~7:53, L267) Rules that break it: "Lastly, not understanding how end of day drawdown works and not understanding the rules. For example, if you make more than $1.5,000 on an eval, then you're breaking the consistency rule. You're going to have to make even more." (wsKx7L9EJys ~9:36, L320)

The "milk prop firms" video (XhsbfEdJBAc, 2026-06-17) gives the same model as a calculator. Base inputs: "Payout rate is 33%. Basically, how many of your funded accounts get to payout and then average payout size is obviously it's just the average payout size. Right now, it's at 4% at 50k. So, it's just $2,000." (XhsbfEdJBAc ~0:49, L58) "Months to payout basically means you eval on day one, then 31 days from now or 22 trading days from then, you will get your first payout." (XhsbfEdJBAc ~0:57, L63) "Cost per account is your cost to achieve a funded account. Basically, three times of a hundred dollars gives you three hundred dollars, and then annualized income is just this number here multiplied by twelve." (XhsbfEdJBAc ~2:09, L104) From $1,000: "If you have a thousand dollar bank, then obviously ten accounts, right? And you can expect to make two thousand one hundred seventy eight dollars back from this expense." (XhsbfEdJBAc ~2:40, L122) and, compounding, "33% pass rate, 10 accounts, you'll do 100 into 2.5K. You'll turn 100 into 2200 in 3 weeks. Then you have 2200, you can buy 22. Now you turn that into 4.8K, right? Take that, put it back in. And then 3 months in, you turn a thousand dollars into ten thousand dollars." (XhsbfEdJBAc ~12:57, L477) His own figures today: "If I trade 50K accounts, which I don't. I do about 0.75, so it's about 3 weeks from eval to getting a payout. Uh I can buy 100 per month, 33% pass rate, 50% payout rate." (XhsbfEdJBAc ~12:05, L447)

The "$30,000 a month" video (0uAQUHEB_L8, 2026-08-16) scales the same arithmetic from $20,000, and he says the capital only changes the account count: "Let's start with 20,000. I know it probably seems like a lot, and it definitely is, but the same applies to $2,000 for your ultimate goal" (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~3:15, L148) "if you don't have 20k to start with, that's okay, because it's the same expected value as 2,000 to 6k, right?" (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~21:36, L802) With few accounts, be pickier: "If you have fewer accounts, you could obviously choose better configurations. I try to go through a lot of accounts every day, so I use slightly lower quality settings." (0uAQUHEB_L8 ~21:48, L809)

## 3. The path, in stages

| Stage | What he says to do | His numbers | Code |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Prove the pass rate | Backtest or paper-trade the +3k-before-2k hit rate on a 50k account under the firm's rules; do not backtest a live equity curve | needs about 33%; "a 1% edge corresponds to roughly a 32% approval rate" | `python -m fpt.cli evaluation --firm lucid_100k_flex` (or a 50k preset) after `backtest` on your own NQ data |
| 1. First evaluations | One cheap firm (he names Lucid 50k Flex), evaluations attacked aggressively, risk capped at the fee | $100 per evaluation, 3 attempts per pass on average, $300 per funded account | `growth --his-stats --start-cash 500` |
| 2. First payout | Same strategy and risk on the funded account, one trade per account per day, five winning days of $150, withdraw 50% | about 1 month from purchase to first payout; $1,500 to $2,000 per payout | `growth --start-cash 500` |
| 3. Reinvest | Every payout buys more evaluations; a daily profit target and a hard daily stop on every account; no copier until the bankroll is about $10,000 | 3x per cycle at a 33% pass rate: $500, $1,500, $4,500, $13,500 | `growth --scan` |
| 4. Add firms | Five funded accounts per firm forces a second, third and fourth firm (he lists MyFundedFutures Flex 50K, Topstep Standard 50K, Alpha Premium 50K, Tradeify Select 50K as near-identical rule sets) | 10-15 funded accounts; 22 funded accounts need more than four firms | `DEFAULT_LADDER` in `fpt/bootstrap.py` |
| 5. His scale | Larger accounts (150k), more firms, lower risk per trade as balances grow, 20-30 trades a day as one trade per account | 40-45 accounts; evaluations aggressive, funded accounts slower | `portfolio --routing round_robin --eval-risk-mode two_trade` |

## 4. What the simulators say about the small-bankroll start

Two independent models, both in `fpt/bootstrap.py`:

* `growth --his-stats` is his calculator turned into a bankroll path: $100 evaluations, pass rate and payout rate as inputs, $2,000 payouts, a one-month cycle, everything reinvested. The expected value per evaluation at his base case (33% / 33% / $2,000) is about +$193, so the edge is real at the account level. What the bankroll decides is whether you survive long enough to realise it.
* `growth` (trade-level) runs the same firm rules as the portfolio simulator with his two-trade evaluation posture and the cheapest-first ladder.

His calculator, 12 months, payouts reinvested, income taken only once cash exceeds $10,000 (5,000 paths; `python -m fpt.cli growth --his-stats --scan --months 12`):

| pass = payout rate | start $250 | $500 | $1,000 | $2,000 | $5,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25% (EV +$50 per eval) | bust 93% | bust 85% | bust 71% | bust 49% | bust 16% |
| 33% (EV +$193) | bust 82% | bust 60% | bust 37% | bust 13%, month-12 payouts median $19.8k | bust 0.5%, median $19.8k |
| 40% (EV +$380) | bust 70% | bust 42% | bust 17% | bust 4%, median $30.6k | bust 0%, median $30.6k |

Trade-level model, 12 months, his edge as measured by third parties (54% / 1.5R, 1.5 qualifying trades a day), two-trade evaluation posture vs a fixed $500 evaluation risk (`growth --scan`):

| posture | $250 | $500 | $1,000 | $2,500 | $5,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| his two-trade | bust 72% | bust 57% | bust 42% | bust 23% | bust 16% |
| fixed $500 risk | bust 65% | bust 48% | bust 33% | bust 14% | bust 9% |

At a more conservative 52% / 1.0 trades a day the two-trade posture busts 84% / 72% / 63% / 45% / 33% of the time across the same bankrolls; at his own 57.5% sample with $100 a month added it busts 55% / 44% / 29% / 17% / 10%.

Reading the tables: the compounding he describes is real in the model, and once a handful of accounts are funded the base grows fast (the medians that are not zero are the survivors). But below about $2,000 the first few evaluations decide everything, and his own "$500 becomes $13,500" is the survivor's path, not the median. Three things move the odds in your favour more than anything else: a higher measured pass rate (the 33% to 40% step halves the bust rate), a cheaper evaluation (his point about buying the cheapest challenges), and adding even $100 a month of outside money while the base is under ten accounts. The payout dollars in the survivor columns are upper bounds, as the README calibration explains; the bust probabilities and time-to-funded are the robust outputs.

## 5. Measure before buying

The cheapest possible start is the backtest he describes, which costs nothing: `python -m fpt.cli backtest --csv data/nq_1min.csv --report report.md --trades trades.csv` on real NQ 1-minute data, then `python -m fpt.cli evaluation --firm <preset>` to turn the trade distribution into a pass probability under the firm's drawdown rule, and `python -m fpt.cli growth --his-stats --pass-rate <yours> --payout-rate <yours> --start-cash <yours>`. If the measured pass rate on a 50k rule set is under about 30%, the tables above say the bankroll needs to be several thousand dollars or the edge needs work first; if it is 33% or better, a $1,000 to $2,000 start has even odds or better of surviving to the compounding stage.
